Four opt-in options change how dragging a group around the grid feels. They all default to `false`, so existing
layouts keep their current behaviour until you turn them on.

<CodeRunner id="dockview/dnd-branch-drops" />

## Collapse the source while dragging

By default the dragged group stays where it is until it is dropped, so the drop overlay is measured against a layout
that still contains it. Once the drop lands the source is removed, the layout reflows, and the group can end up
somewhere other than where the overlay pointed.

With `dndCollapseSource` the dragged group is collapsed out of the grid as soon as the drag starts. The layout reflows
straight away, the overlay is measured against the layout the drop will produce, and the group lands exactly where the
overlay showed. Cancelling the drag puts the group back at its size.

It applies when dragging a whole group by its header, or the only tab of a group. Floating groups, popout windows and
edge groups are left in place, as is the last group in the grid.

## Drop beside a row or column

An edge drop normally splits the group under the cursor. With `dndBranchDrops` it can also apply to a row or column
around that group. For example, with two groups stacked in a column, dropping on the right of the top group can place
the dragged group to the right of the whole column, at its full height.

Along a group's edge, a thin band per enclosing row or column (at most 24px each) targets those regions: the band closest
to the edge targets the largest enclosing row or column whose edge is that group's edge. The rest of the edge zone
targets the group itself. The overlay outlines
the region the drop applies to and previews the half of it the dropped group will take.

The overlay and drop events report how far up the layout the drop applies through `depth`: `0` is the group itself,
`1` the row or column around it, and so on. Use it in `onWillShowOverlay` or `onWillDrop` to refine or veto a drop:

```ts
api.onWillShowOverlay((event) => {
    // Allow row and column drops, but never stacking into a group.
    if (event.kind === 'content' && event.position === 'center') {
        event.preventDefault();
    }
});

api.onWillDrop((event) => {
    console.log(event.position, event.depth);
});
```

## Open room instead of drawing the overlay

With `dndLivePreview`, an edge drop is not previewed with the overlay. The groups around it make way instead, opening
an empty slot exactly where the dragged group will land and at the size it will take. Dropped beside the other groups
of a row or column, the group takes an equal share of it, the others giving up room in proportion to their sizes: three
equal groups stay equal. Dropped beside a single group across its row or column, the two share its room equally. The slot stays put until another
drop is previewed, so the room it opens never moves the target out from under the cursor, and releasing anywhere in the
layout drops into it. Dragging out of the layout closes it again.

It applies to edge drops on grid groups, including drops beside a row or column, and to the outer layout edges: there
too the room opens in first or last place across the whole layout, in place of the edge overlay. Stacking drops onto a
group keep the overlay. With `dndBranchDrops`, a wider band along the layout's own border (48px) drops across the whole
layout, so the first and last places are easy to reach.

## Drop on the nearest side

By default a group only takes an edge drop near that edge, and its middle drops into the group. With
`dndNearestEdge`, the pointer drops on whichever side of the group is nearest, measured in proportion to the group's
size: the group's diagonals split it into four triangles, so there is no zone to avoid in the middle, even on a long,
narrow group. Once the pointer is on a side, that side is kept until another one is clearly nearer, so the drop does
not flip back and forth around a diagonal. Dropping into a group to stack is then done on its tabs. With
`dndBranchDrops`, the rows and columns around the group only take a thin band along its very edge; the rest of each side
drops on the group itself.

This applies to the content of groups; the outer layout edges and the tab strip keep their behaviour. An app's own
`dropPositionResolver` takes precedence. The resolver is also available on its own as `nearestEdgeResolver`, to build
on in a custom resolver.

columns on offer, and the room opened for the drop, are those of the layout the dragged group leaves behind.
